# Writing

A note is read later by someone who was not there when it was written, often its owner months on. These lines cover what takes understanding of the content to judge. A document type's required headings and templates stay as they are, and the destination vault's live policy overrides any line here. These lines shape prose the task already asked for; they are not a reason to rewrite what it did not.

## Revising

- Line-edit prose, preserving the author's voice and meaning.

## Quotation and attribution

- Put quoted source text, its translation, its interpretation and the writer's own words in separate paragraphs or under short labels (`번역`, `해석`, `내 말`).
- Keep a translation out of the blockquote that holds the original, and mark it as a translation.
- Present a sentence as a quotation only when its source is named and contains it word for word.

## A note that stands alone

- Write the body as the canonical text for a reader who never saw the conversation, naming the people who spoke or decided and leaving out the conversation that produced the note.
- Put uncertainty next to the content it qualifies ("amount unconfirmed"), once.
- Let the reader tell observation, assumption and interpretation apart.
- Prefer the primary source, and mark a claim whose source could not be checked as unchecked.

## Shape and terms

- Write a heading that predicts its content, and keep under it only what answers the same question.
- Use a list for steps and options, prose for an argument, and a table when several things are compared on several criteria.
- Bold only what a reader scans for, such as a key term, a decision or a warning.
- Use one term for one concept throughout, following the existing page's title.
- Link a note only after reading the target confirms it supports what the sentence says, and create no page just to have a link target.
